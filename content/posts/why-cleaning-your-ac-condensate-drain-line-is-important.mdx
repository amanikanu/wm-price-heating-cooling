## **Importance of Cleaning Your Condensate Drain Line**

A condensate drain line, drains moisture collected from the removal of excess [humidity](/products/carrier/) while the air conditioner is operating, conditioning and cooling the air in your home. The moisture will travel through the drain line and drip outside.

It is important to clean, flush, and unclog your condensate drain line. A clogged drain line can allow microbial growth on your evaporator coils or lead to a complete system breakdown.

## **How Do Condensate Drain Lines Clog?**

Air Conditioning condensate drains often clog due to a combination of moisture, debris, and lack of maintenance. The constant presence of water in the drain line creates and ideal environment for algae, mildew, and microbial growth which can eventually block the pipe. Dust and dirt from the air handler-especially when the air filter is dirty or missing-can also accumulate in the condensate pan and drain line. In older systems, rust and corrosion from metal parts can flake off and contribute to clogs. Additionally, if the drain line is not installed with the proper slope, water may not flow correctly, increasing the risk of buildup. Without regular maintenance to flush or clean the line, these issues can easily lead to a clog and potential water damage.

## **How to Prevent Condensate Drain Line Clogs**

The best way to prevent your condensate drain line from clogging is by scheduling routine professional maintenance! Some actions you can take on your own to ensure your system continues to operate smoothly and efficiently include changing your filter every 30 to 90 days and pouring distilled vinegar down your condensate drain line to help remove and clear out any debris.
