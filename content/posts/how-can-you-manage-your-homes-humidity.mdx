Indoor humidity is important for comfort. It makes all the difference in how comfortable your home is while you are relaxing, sleeping, and keeping you or your family healthy.

Indoor humidity can affect your health, by triggering things such as asthma attacks or flaring allergies and tends to put everyone in a sluggish mood. There are ways to manage the relative humidity inside your [Warren](/city/warren-oh/) home year-round to keep you comfortable and healthy.

## **How to Manage Your Home’s Humidity**

First of all let us answer the main question: *what humidity level do I want in my* *Warren home?*

The answer is going to be different for every person! It’s like the temperature—some like it hot and others like it cold. Typically, you want your indoor humidity levels between 30 and 50 percent for ideal comfort.

According to the US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), maintaining a safe indoor humidity level to below 60 percent helps control the growth of mold and reduces potential heath problems associated with mold exposures such as allergic reactions, asthma and other respiratory issues. A relatively inexpensive instrument called a hygrometer can measure the indoor relative humidity at your home, however, most modern thermostats also measure and display the indoor relative humidity.

## **What Is** **Dehumidification?**

Dehumidification is the removal or moisture from the air in your home. Your [air conditioner](/products/carrier/) (AC) removes humidity from the air, but this is not always sufficient to maintain ideal humidity levels in the home. The best recommendation for managing the excessive humidity levels in your home is to install a whole-house dehumidifier.

This device can work with your [heating](/residential/furnaces/), ventilation, and [air conditioning](/residential/air-conditioning/) (HVAC) equipment or as a standalone unit to remove moisture from the air before it circulates through your home.

A dehumidifier helps to keep your home comfortable all summer long. It operates similarly to your AC unit in that moisture is collected by a refrigeration coil within the device. The excessive moisture is then drained and the conditioned air is distributed throughout the home.

Signs that you have too much humidity at home:

- Moist or sticky air
- Damp or musty smell
- Condensation on your windows
- Wet walls
- Bacterial growth (in either the home, attic, or crawlspace)

Too much humidity in the air can cause:

- Poor [indoor air quality](/residential/indoor-air-quality/) that could lead to illness
- Mold, mildew, and bacteria growth
- Worse or more frequent symptoms for asthma or allergy sufferers
- Damage to your home from extensive exposure to moisture

## **What Is Humidification?**

Humidification is adding moisture to the air. During the colder months, the cold outdoor air holds less moisture. As it enters the home through infiltration, heating it further dries it out. There are a few different options for whole-house humidifier units.

These units work with your heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) equipment to add the needed moisture from the air as it circulates through your home. The three main options are a by-pass humidifier, fan powered, or steam humidifier.

A properly designed and installed humidification system can make a significant difference in a home during the heating season, but only if designed and installed properly. A poorly installed unit can cause a world of headaches for the unsuspecting homeowner. Improper humidification and design can lead to mold or bacterial growth throughout the home or attic. Equipment failure due to poor installation can lead to leaks and damage ceilings, wall, insulation, framing, or even roofing.

Signs that you do not have enough humidity at home:

- Asthma and allergy symptoms worsen
- Dry skin and chapped lips
- Wooden floors or furniture which dries out and cracks
- Your home never feels as warm as the [thermostat](/products/accessories/) indicates

A lack of humidity can cause:

- Sickness to spread more easily
- Home damage from dry/cracked wood
- Increase in utility bills
